Quick Answer

Keep your golf cart running with four habits: charge the battery after every use, check tire pressure monthly (18–22 psi), wash off salt and sand weekly, and store it dry with a charged battery. Electric carts need minimal maintenance — no oil changes, no gas — so these simple steps cover most of what a cart owner needs to do. If you're visiting Destin, skip the maintenance entirely: every cart we rent is brand new and serviced before delivery.

Battery Care: The #1 Maintenance Task

Your battery is the heart of an electric cart. Charge after every use — even short trips — and avoid letting it sit below 50% charge for long periods. Plug in overnight with the charger that came with your cart, and never leave it plugged in for weeks at a time without checking it.

Tire Pressure and Tread

Most golf cart tires run 18–22 psi — check the sidewall for the exact recommendation. Under-inflated tires drain battery faster and wear unevenly. Check monthly, and look for cracks or bald spots, especially on carts that sit in the Florida sun.

Cleaning: Salt, Sand, and Sun

Coastal air is tough on carts. Rinse off salt and sand after beach trips, wipe down seats and dashboards, and use a UV protectant on vinyl seats. Keep the battery compartment dry and free of debris.

Seasonal Storage

If you store a cart for the season: clean it, inflate tires to the top of the recommended range, store in a dry covered space, and keep the battery at 50–100% charge with a maintenance charger. In spring, check tire pressure, brakes, and battery water levels before your first ride.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should you charge a golf cart battery?

Charge after every use, even short trips, and avoid letting the battery drop below 50% for long periods. Plug in overnight to keep it ready and extend battery life.

What tire pressure should a golf cart have?

Most golf cart tires should be inflated to 18–22 psi. Check the sidewall for the manufacturer's recommendation and check monthly.

How do you store a golf cart for the winter?

Clean the cart, inflate tires, store in a dry place, and keep the battery at 50–100% charge with a maintenance charger. Cover the cart to protect it from dust and moisture.
