Electric vs Gas Golf Carts: Pros, Cons, and Costs Compared

Which type is right for you?

Quick Answer

For most people, electric golf carts are the better choice: they cost less to run and maintain, run quietly, and produce zero fumes. Gas carts still make sense for very large properties, long daily distances, or steep terrain. On a Destin or Miramar Beach vacation, electric is a clear winner — our fleet is 100% electric, and rentals start at just $99/day.

Running Costs

Electric carts cost pennies per mile to charge. Gas carts need regular fill-ups and their fuel economy is measured in miles per gallon, not miles per kilowatt-hour — over a season of daily use, electric typically saves hundreds of dollars. Battery replacement is the big-ticket item on an electric cart, but it happens every few years, not every month.

Maintenance

Electric carts have far fewer moving parts: no engine, no oil changes, no air filters, no fuel system to winterize. Gas carts demand oil changes, filter swaps, and occasional carburetor attention. For owners who don't love wrenching, electric wins easily.

Performance and Range

Modern electric carts cover 25–45 miles per charge — more than enough for a day of cruising Destin and Miramar Beach — and deliver instant torque for a responsive ride. Gas carts offer quick refueling and strong hill-climbing, which matters mainly on large or mountainous properties.

Noise, Fumes, and the Environment

Electric carts are quiet enough to carry on a conversation at full speed, produce no exhaust, and are gentle on the environment — a real plus on the beach. Gas carts are louder and smell like fuel. On vacation, electric keeps the vibe relaxed and the air clean.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better: electric or gas golf cart?

For most owners and renters, electric golf carts are better: they cost less to run and maintain, are quieter, and produce no fumes. Gas carts only make sense for large properties, long daily distances, or very hilly terrain.

Are electric golf carts cheaper to maintain than gas?

Yes. Electric carts have far fewer moving parts — no engine, no oil changes, no fuel system. The main cost is battery replacement every few years.

How far can an electric golf cart go on a charge?

Most modern electric golf carts travel 25–45 miles per charge — far more than a typical day of beach cruising in Destin or Miramar Beach. Charging overnight restores full range.
