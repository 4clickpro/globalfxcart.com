Golf Club Maintenance in Salt Air: Keeping Your Set Clean on the Emerald Coast

Coastal air is rough on golf equipment. A five-minute post-round routine keeps grips tacky, grooves clean, and rust off your Emerald Coast set.

Living or vacationing on the Emerald Coast means salt in the air — and salt is quietly working on your golf clubs between rounds. The good news: five minutes of care after each round keeps a set looking and playing new for years.

The Five-Minute Post-Round Routine

Rinse heads in a bucket of warm water (never soak the hosels), scrub grooves with a soft brush, wipe grips with a damp cloth and mild soap, then dry everything before storage. Clean grooves grip the ball and generate real spin — dirty grooves fly farther and stop less.

Stop Salt Rust Before It Starts

Salt air pits chrome and can spot raw wedges within weeks. A light wipe of household oil on a cloth run over the heads once a month, plus storing clubs indoors — never on a porch or in a beach garage — prevents most of it.

Grips Are the First to Go

Sun and sweat harden grips in a single Florida season. Clean them every few weeks and replace them when they turn glossy — fresh grips are the cheapest performance upgrade in golf.

Store Smart, Ride Smarter

Keep the bag inside, upright, and dry. If you ride to the course in one of our rental carts, use the bag well or rear seat — and take the clubs inside when you park for the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do golf clubs rust in Florida?

They can — salt air accelerates pitting on raw wedges and chrome, especially in beachfront storage. Indoor storage and a monthly oiled wipe-down prevent nearly all of it.

How often should I clean my golf clubs?

A quick wipe after every round and a full clean of heads, grooves, and grips every 3 to 4 rounds — more often in coastal humidity.

Can I leave golf clubs in a golf cart overnight?

Better not — overnight heat, dew, and humidity degrade grips and can loosen epoxy over time. Bring the bag indoors, even for a week-long vacation.
